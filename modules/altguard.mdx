AltGuard holds every new human member behind a verification gate until `verify.moddy.app` — a dedicated external service, not the bot itself — decides whether their account looks like a second account of someone already on your server. The bot never sees or stores any personal data from that process: no fingerprint, no email, no IP address. Everything crossing back to the bot is Discord ids, a verdict, and category labels. The only thing the bot sends the other way is the public id of the account that invited the member, when it can tell (see [Who invited the member](#who-invited-the-member)). See [What AltGuard Checks, and What Happens to That Data](/modules/altguard-data) for exactly what's collected, why, and for how long.

<Warning>
  AltGuard is not a heuristic the bot runs on its own — it relies on an explicit, consent-based check performed by Moddy's verification service in the member's browser. A member must actively go through the verification screen; there is no passive "AI guesses your account age" detection running in the background.
</Warning>

## How It Works

<Steps>
  <Step title="A member joins">
    They immediately receive the **unverified role** and can only see your configured verification channel. Bots skip this entirely — they can't click a button, so they're never gated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They open the verification panel">
    A single permanent message with one button sits in the verification channel. Clicking it opens a consent screen explaining exactly what will be checked (browser and device characteristics, a technical cookie, Discord email, server list, linked accounts such as Steam, Xbox or GitHub, IP address, and the account that invited them — all encrypted or turned into non-reversible fingerprints), with links to the data notice, terms, and privacy policy. Two checkboxes must be ticked to continue; closing the screen sends nothing anywhere.
  </Step>
  <Step title="They authorize on Discord's own screen">
    After consenting, the member gets a personal, single-use link (valid 20 minutes) where the verification service runs its checks. Discord's own authorization screen asks for the member's identity, email address, server list and **linked accounts**. Moddy has no part in this step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The verdict comes back">
    The service returns one of three outcomes: **verified** (the member gets the verified role and moves on to Auto Role, if configured), **pending** (kept behind the gate, awaiting a moderator's manual review), or **denied** (kept behind the gate). The member is DMed the outcome in plain language — never the underlying score or signals.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  AltGuard may occasionally put a legitimate new member on hold who happens to share signals with a previously banned account. Review pending verdicts in your log channel before assuming the worst — that's exactly what the manual `/altguard verify` override below is for.
</Note>

## Enabling AltGuard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the configuration panel">
    Type `/config` in your server and select **AltGuard**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a verification channel">
    This becomes the _only_ channel the unverified role can see. Moddy automatically locks every other channel from that role when you save — you don't need to configure channel permissions by hand.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the unverified and verified roles">
    Both are required for the module to activate. The unverified role holds new members back; the verified role is granted once they pass.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a log channel (recommended)">
    Verdicts and manual staff decisions are posted here. Keep it private to your moderation team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the panel language">
    The verification panel's wording is fixed (every server explains the same data processing to members) — you only choose which of the supported languages it's shown in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Moddy needs the **Manage Roles** and **Manage Channels** permissions, with its own role positioned above both the verified and unverified roles, to lock down channels and hand out roles correctly.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  **Manage Server** is recommended, but optional. It lets Moddy read your invite list to work out which invite a member used (see below). Without it, AltGuard works normally — it just has less information.
</Tip>

## Who invited the member

When Moddy can determine which account invited a member, it passes that account to AltGuard. A second account is often invited through the main account's link, so AltGuard uses it **only to reinforce another link** (a shared device, network or linked third-party account). On its own it counts for nothing: inviting friends is the normal way to join a server.

Moddy can't determine the inviter when the member arrives through your server's vanity URL, through a single-use or since-deleted invite, or when several members join at the same instant.

## VPNs, proxies and private relays

Verification is refused when it's done from a VPN, a proxy or a hosting provider — **including iCloud Private Relay and Cloudflare WARP**. Here's what the member sees:

1. The verification page says a VPN was detected and asks them to turn it off and try again.
2. A **Try again** button works with the same link until it expires (20 minutes).
3. If the link has expired, they start again from the button in the verification channel, as usual (5 attempts maximum per 10 minutes).
4. Until they retry without a VPN they **stay unverified**. This is not a denial — no decision has been made.

<Note>
  An attempt blocked by a VPN produces **no message** in your log channel, because it isn't a verdict. A member who stays stuck with nothing in the logs is most likely behind a VPN. This protection is on by default on every server and can't be turned off from Moddy.
</Note>

## Reviewing and Overriding Verdicts

Pending and denied members stay behind the gate until a moderator steps in. From your log channel, moderators with the **Kick Members** permission (the default — adjustable per role from **Server Settings → Integrations**) can override any verdict directly in Discord:

```discord
/altguard verify <member>
/altguard unverify <member>
```

`/altguard verify` lets a member through manually — granting the verified role and handing them off to Auto Role, if enabled — while `/altguard unverify` sends a previously verified member back behind the gate.

<Tip>
  If you have questions about a specific decision — why a member was held, what score or signals were involved — that detail isn't exposed in `/altguard`. Reach out through the [support server](https://moddy.app/support) with the case's details and the Moddy team can look it up on request.
</Tip>

## Reading the log channel

Each verdict card in the log channel (and the staff command `/mod altguard refusal`) lists the signals that actually weighed on the decision, each with a readable label followed by its code — for example `Same graphics card (gpu_match)`. The list of linked accounts only shows accounts that weighed in the decision; weak matches don't appear. A signal Moddy doesn't recognise yet is shown with its raw code — that isn't an error, AltGuard can add new ones.

<Warning>
  `invited_by` never justifies a sanction on its own — it only reinforces another link. And never show the score, the signals or the linked accounts to the member being verified.
</Warning>

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `cookie_match` | Same browser (AltGuard cookie) |
| `email_match` | Same email address |
| `connection_match` **new** | Same linked third-party account (Steam, Xbox, GitHub…) |
| `visitor_match` | Same browser fingerprint |
| `render_match` | Same graphics and audio rendering |
| `gpu_match` | Same graphics card |
| `hardware_match` | Same hardware setup (screen, CPU, timezone, model) |
| `fonts_match` | Same installed fonts |
| `ip_match` | Same IP address |
| `ip_prefix_match` | Same subnet |
| `asn_match` | Same network operator |
| `guild_overlap` | Discord servers in common |
| `guild_overlap_high` | Many Discord servers in common |
| `join_burst` | Joined the server less than 15 min apart |
| `invited_by` **new** | Invited by this account (reinforces another link) |
| `account_new_24h` | Account created less than 24 h ago |
| `account_new_7d` | Account created less than 7 days ago |
| `account_new_30d` | Account created less than 30 days ago |
| `email_unverified` | Email not verified on Discord |
| `email_disposable` | Disposable email |
| `default_avatar` | Default avatar |
| `no_badge` | No badge |
| `datacenter_ip` | Connection from a host, cloud or VPN |
| `vpn_detected` **new** | VPN unmasked: the real IP behind it was found |

## Troubleshooting

<Accordion title="A member's verification doesn't go through">
  The most common cause is a VPN, proxy or private relay (iCloud Private Relay and Cloudflare WARP included). Ask them to turn it off, then click **Try again** on the verification page, or start the verification again from the panel button. See [VPNs, proxies and private relays](#vpns-proxies-and-private-relays).
</Accordion>

## Auto Role Integration

If your server also uses the **Auto Role** feature, AltGuard holds it back automatically: a member who hasn't passed the gate gets no auto role, no matter what. The moment they pass — automatically or via a manual `/altguard verify` — Auto Role runs as normal.
