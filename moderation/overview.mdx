## How Moderation Works

Every time a moderator issues a sanction — or AutoMod acts on a message — Moddy creates or updates a **case**. Each case is assigned a unique **6-character reference code** (for example, `AB3XR7`) that lets anyone with the right permissions pull up the full record instantly.

Inside a case you'll find a **chronological timeline** of every event related to that member: sanctions applied, sanctions revoked, notes left by moderators, and evidence attachments. This makes it easy to see the full history of an interaction at a glance, whether you're reviewing a single incident or auditing a pattern of behaviour.

Cases stay open as long as at least one sanction is active, and close automatically when all sanctions expire or are revoked. Moderators can also manually close or reopen a case at any time.

## Who Can Moderate

Case management in your server happens through the `/cases` command, gated by Discord's **Manage Messages** permission (or Administrator) by default — adjustable per role from **Server Settings → Integrations**. Within `/cases`, adding a ban additionally requires **Ban Members**, and adding a warn or mute requires **Timeout Members**, so a moderator only sees the actions their own Discord permissions allow. Any member — with no permission requirement — can view their own moderation history across every server with `/mycases`, but only their own, and read-only.

<Note>
  Moddy automatically records bans and timeouts that are applied directly through Discord — even when Moddy commands aren't used. This means your case history stays complete even if a moderator uses the native Discord moderation tools instead.
</Note>

## Appeals

When AutoMod sanctions a member, Moddy sends that member a **direct message** containing an appeal button. The member can submit an appeal without leaving Discord, and it is routed to your server's moderators for review.

Moderators can resolve an appeal in one of three ways:

- **Accept** — the sanction is revoked and the case is updated accordingly.
- **Refuse** — the sanction stands and the member is notified.
- **Transform** — the original sanction is replaced with a different one (for example, downgrading a ban to a mute).

All appeal outcomes are recorded in the case timeline so there is always a clear audit trail.
